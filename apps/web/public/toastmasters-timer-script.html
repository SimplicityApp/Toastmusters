Toastmasters Timer Script – What to Say, Word for Word

Last updated 3 October 2026

As the Timer (or timekeeper) you speak twice. At the start you explain the signals — green at the minimum time, yellow at the midpoint, red at the maximum, plus a 30-second grace period — in about 45 seconds. At the end you deliver the timing report: each speaker's name, their allowed range, their exact time, and whether they qualified for an award. Both scripts are below, ready to read.

Most new Timers are not worried about running a stopwatch. They are worried about the two moments when the whole club is listening to them. This page gives you the exact wording for both, plus a log template to fill in while you time. Print it, or keep it open on a second screen next to the Toastmasters Timer app.

Script 1: Explaining the Signals

The Toastmaster of the Day will introduce you near the start of the meeting, usually right after the other functionary roles. Stand, face the room, and keep this to under a minute. Guests are the real audience here — members already know the system.

"Thank you, Toastmaster. Good evening everyone, and a special welcome to our guests. My job tonight is to time each speaker and make sure we finish on schedule.

I use three signals. Green means you have reached the minimum time for your segment — you are now in the qualifying zone. Yellow means you are at the midpoint of your time range and should start moving toward your conclusion. Red means you have reached the maximum time and should wrap up promptly.

You have a 30-second grace period on either side of your time range. If you finish more than 30 seconds before the green signal or more than 30 seconds after the red, you will not be eligible for tonight's award.

I will record everyone's exact time and read the full timing report near the end of the meeting. Back to you, Toastmaster."

Two optional additions, depending on your club. If your club uses a physical timing device rather than cards, say where it is: "The signal lights are on the table to my left — please glance over during your speech." If you are running an online meeting, say how the signal appears: "I will be timing from my video window. My background will change to green, yellow, and red — you may want to pin my video so you can see it clearly." See how to run the Timer role on Zoom for the full online setup.

The script above uses the contest rule: 30 seconds of grace on either side, except Table Topics, which must last at least 1:00. Clubs set their own rule for meeting awards, so check yours first. If your club requires speakers to reach green, say instead: "If you finish before the green signal, or more than 30 seconds after the red, you will not be eligible for tonight's award."

Script 2: Delivering the Timing Report

Near the end of the meeting, the Toastmaster of the Day or General Evaluator will call for your report. Read it by segment rather than in the order things happened — it is easier for members to follow when they are about to vote.

"Thank you, Toastmaster. Here is tonight's timing report.

Prepared speeches, with a range of 5 to 7 minutes. Maria Alvarez spoke for 6 minutes 12 seconds — within time. David Chen spoke for 7 minutes 22 seconds — within time, using the grace period. Priya Nair spoke for 4 minutes 21 seconds — under the minimum, and therefore not eligible for the award.

Table Topics, with a range of 1 to 2 minutes. Sam Okafor, 1 minute 34 seconds — within time. Lena Fischer, 2 minutes 41 seconds — over time and not eligible. Tom Bailey, 1 minute 02 seconds — within time.

Evaluations, with a range of 2 to 3 minutes. All three evaluators — Maria, Sam, and Priya — finished within time.

That is seven of nine speakers within time tonight. Back to you, Toastmaster."

Notice what the script does not do. It does not say "David really pushed it there" or "Lena went way over." It reports numbers and eligibility, nothing more. Evaluating the speech is the Evaluator's job; your job is the clock. A neutral report also spares members the embarrassment of a public critique they did not sign up for.

Handling awkward moments

  • A speaker you forgot to start. Say so plainly and move on: "I was unable to record an accurate time for Jane's speech, so I will not report a time for that segment." Do not guess a number — a fabricated time can hand someone an award they did not earn, or take one away.
  • A speaker who is disqualified by a second or two. Report the number without softening it, but without dwelling on it either. "2 minutes 31 seconds — one second past the grace period, so not eligible tonight." The rule is the rule, and stating it evenly is kinder than an apologetic ramble.
  • A segment that was not on the agenda. If the Toastmaster adds an unplanned timed item, ask for the time range before it starts. If you were not told, report it as untimed rather than applying a range you assumed.

Skip the Paper Log Entirely

The log above works, but writing times by hand while watching a speaker and operating a stopwatch is genuinely difficult — especially during Table Topics, where speakers follow each other with no gap. The Toastmasters Timer app keeps the log for you: enter the names from the agenda before the meeting, pick the speech type, and press start. It records each speaker's exact time, flags who is inside and outside the range, and produces the report at the end so you can read it straight off the screen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do you say as the Timer in Toastmasters?

At the start of the meeting you explain the three signals and the grace period, in under a minute. At the end you read the timing report: each speaker's name, allowed range, exact time, and award eligibility. Both scripts are above and can be read word for word.

How long should the Timer's explanation be?

Thirty to sixty seconds. Name the three signals, mention the 30-second grace period, and say you will report times later. Do not recite the timing for every speech type — the Toastmaster of the Day announces each speech type as it comes up, and listing them all makes the introduction drag.

What goes in a Toastmasters timing report?

For every timed segment: the speaker's name, the allowed range, the exact time in minutes and seconds, and whether they were within time. Close with a count of how many speakers qualified. Keep it factual — no commentary on the speech itself.

Does the Timer report Table Topics separately?

Yes. Group the report by segment — prepared speeches, then Table Topics, then evaluations. Members vote by category, so a report organised the same way is much easier to follow.

What is the 30-second grace period?

Speakers may run up to 30 seconds past the red signal and still qualify for awards. A 5–7 minute speech qualifies at 7:29 and is disqualified at 7:31. In contests the same 30 seconds applies below the minimum, so that speech must last at least 4:30. Table Topics is the exception and must last at least 1:00. For meeting awards, some clubs instead require speakers to reach green, so check your club's rule. Because the rule turns on single seconds, record exact times rather than rounding.

What if I am the Timer and also have another role?

Common in small clubs. Tell the Toastmaster of the Day in advance so they can pause between segments while you switch tasks. If you are speaking yourself, hand the timer to another member for that segment — you cannot time your own speech.
