Best Toastmasters Timer Apps in 2026

Last updated 10 August 2026

There is no single best Toastmasters timer — the right one depends on your meeting format. For in-person meetings, almost any free web or mobile timer works, and physical cards are still perfectly valid. For Zoom meetings, pick a timer that switches your virtual background automatically. For clubs that live inside the Toastmasters International website, the official Meeting Tools timer keeps everything in one account. All the major web options are free.

We build one of the tools listed here, so treat this as an informed comparison rather than a neutral one. What follows is an honest account of what each option does well — including the cases where another tool is the better choice.

Toastmasters International Meeting Tools

Toastmasters International offers a timer inside the Meeting Tools section of its member site. It sits alongside the other meeting utilities and requires signing in with a member profile.

Where it wins: it is official, which matters to clubs that prefer to standardise on organisation-provided tools, and it needs no explanation to officers who already use the member site. Toastmasters International also publishes free branded timing backgrounds for Zoom in a number of languages — English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Arabic, Tamil, and both Chinese scripts — which is far broader language coverage than any third-party tool offers.

Where it falls short: it requires a member login, so a guest or a brand-new member handed the Timer role at short notice may not be able to open it. It is a general-purpose timer rather than a Zoom-integrated one, so online meetings still need a separate solution for displaying the signal.

Toastmost Timer

Toastmost provides a browser-based speech timer that is designed to pair with the WordPress for Toastmasters club-website platform, and it can also be used standalone. It offers modes intended for online meetings.

Where it wins: if your club already runs its website on Toastmost or WordPress for Toastmasters, the timer is part of a system your officers know, and club-specific timer pages can live on your own domain. It has been around a long time and is well established in the community.

Where it falls short: the tight integration with one platform is less of an advantage for clubs that use something else, and there is no Zoom app that manipulates your virtual background directly.

Speech Timer for Toastmasters (Caleb Grove)

A long-standing, straightforward web timer for Toastmasters-format speeches. Its notable technical feature is that it keeps working offline once you have opened it recently in an up-to-date browser.

Where it wins: offline capability is a genuine differentiator, and it is the reason to pick this one. Plenty of clubs meet in church halls, community centres, and back rooms of restaurants where the Wi-Fi is unreliable or absent. A timer that keeps running when the connection drops mid-meeting solves a real problem. It is also refreshingly simple — there is very little to learn.

Where it falls short: simplicity cuts both ways. There is no Zoom integration and less in the way of agenda or reporting features, so online clubs and Timers who want their report generated for them will want something else.

Mobile Timer Apps

Several native apps exist on iOS and Android, including TM Timer on Google Play and options such as ToastBuster Timer and Speech Timer for Toastmasters on the App Store. Most let you enter speakers in advance and change the screen colour at each threshold.

Where they win: a phone is the device you definitely have with you, they run without a connection, and holding a phone screen up is a natural in-person signal. Several include per-speaker agenda entry.

Where they fall short: a phone screen is small, which limits how visible the signal is from across a room — this matters most in larger clubs. They also require installation, so handing the role to someone at the last minute means asking them to download an app first. And in an online meeting a phone timer cannot drive what appears in your Zoom window.

Printed Timing Cards

Worth including honestly: for a small in-person club, a set of green, yellow, and red cards and any stopwatch is a complete solution. It has no batteries, no login, no software updates, and no failure modes beyond forgetting the cards.

Where they fall short: you still need something to track exact times and produce the report, cards do not travel to online meetings, and a card held to a webcam is much harder to see than a full-screen colour.

Toastmasters Timer (This Site)

Our tool is a free browser timer that also installs as a Zoom app. Its distinguishing feature is that in Zoom it changes the Timer's virtual background to green, yellow, and red automatically as each threshold is reached, with no clicking through Zoom's settings mid-speech.

Where it wins: online and hybrid meetings, particularly Table Topics, where speakers follow each other with almost no gap and manual background switching cannot keep up. It ships with the standard timing rules preloaded for each speech type, logs every speaker's exact time, and generates the timing report so you are not writing on paper while operating the clock. It needs no account.

Where it falls short: it is a web tool, so it wants an internet connection — if your venue's Wi-Fi is unreliable, Caleb Grove's offline timer or a native mobile app is the safer choice. It is also English-only at present, where Toastmasters International's own backgrounds cover eleven languages.

How to Choose

Your club meets on Zoom or in hybrid format

Use a timer that drives the virtual background automatically. This is the case where the difference between tools is largest, because the alternative — clicking through Zoom settings three times per speaker — genuinely fails during fast segments. Add the Zoom timer, or if you prefer switching manually, download the free green, yellow, and red backgrounds.

Your club meets in person with good Wi-Fi

Almost anything works. Pick whichever tool your Timers find easiest to open, and prioritise a large, bright display over feature count. A laptop turned toward the speaker beats a phone in a big room.

Your venue's internet is unreliable

Choose an offline-capable tool — Caleb Grove's Speech Timer or a native mobile app. This is a real constraint and it should override every other consideration; a timer that dies mid-meeting is worse than cards.

Your club runs everything through toastmasters.org

Use the official Meeting Tools timer. Fewer tools means fewer things to explain to each new officer, and the official backgrounds cover far more languages than any third-party option.

You are the Timer tonight and have five minutes to prepare

Open any free browser timer — no installation, no login. Then read the timer script so you know what to say, and check the timing chart for the thresholds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best timer for Toastmasters meetings?

It depends on format. In person, any free timer with the standard colour signals works. On Zoom, use one that changes your virtual background automatically. If your club is standardised on toastmasters.org, the official Meeting Tools timer is the tidiest fit.

Is there an official Toastmasters timer?

Yes — Toastmasters International provides one in Meeting Tools on its member site, plus free branded timing backgrounds for Zoom in eleven languages.

Do I need to pay for a Toastmasters timer?

No. Every major web-based option is free, including the official tools, Toastmost, Caleb Grove's timer, and this one. Some native mobile apps charge a small one-off fee or show ads.

Which Toastmasters timer works offline?

Caleb Grove's Speech Timer keeps working offline after a recent page load, and native mobile apps run without a connection. Browser tools that need a fresh page load require internet at the start of the meeting.

How do I time speeches in a Zoom meeting?

Switch your virtual background between green, yellow, and red, or use a Zoom app that switches them for you. Holding cards to a webcam works for prepared speeches but not for Table Topics.

Can two people share the timing role?

Yes, and contests require it: two timers, one holding the stopwatch and keeping the official written record, the other operating the signalling device. Plenty of clubs borrow the same split for ordinary meetings, with one person signalling and the other recording times. See the contest timing rules.
