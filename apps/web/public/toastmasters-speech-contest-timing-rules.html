Toastmasters Speech Contest Timing Rules

Last updated 10 August 2026 · Based on the 2026–2027 Speech Contest Rulebook

Contest timing is stricter than club timing and the thresholds differ per contest. International, Humorous, and Online contests run 5–7 minutes with disqualification under 4:30 or over 7:30. Table Topics runs 1–2 minutes (DQ under 1:00 or over 2:30). Evaluation runs 2–3 minutes (DQ under 1:30 or over 3:30). Tall Tales runs 3–5 minutes (DQ under 2:30 or over 5:30). Timers must not consider fractions of a second.

Disqualification takes effect one second past the stated threshold. A 5–7 minute speech is still qualifying at 7:30 and is disqualified at 7:31. Because timers must not consider fractions of a second, a stopwatch reading of 7:30.9 is recorded as 7:30 and the contestant survives.

Source. The figures on this page are taken from the Toastmasters International Speech Contest Rulebook (Item 1171) for 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, Rule 6. The rulebook is revised annually and is the only authority that counts on contest day — download the current edition from toastmasters.org and check it before you serve as a contest timer. How Long Each Signal Stays Up

Contest rules specify not just when each colour appears but how long it is displayed — a detail club meetings usually ignore. Signals must be clearly visible to the speaker but not obvious to the audience.

  • International, Humorous, Online: green at 5:00 held for one minute, yellow at 6:00 held for one minute, red at 7:00 held until the speech concludes.
  • Evaluation: green at 2:00 for 30 seconds, yellow at 2:30 for 30 seconds, red at 3:00 until the evaluation concludes.
  • Table Topics: green at 1:00 for 30 seconds, yellow at 1:30 for 30 seconds, red at 2:00 until the speech concludes.
  • Tall Tales: green at 3:00 for one minute, yellow at 4:00 for one minute, red at 5:00 until the speech concludes.

One rule catches new contest timers out: no signal may be given for the overtime period. Once red is showing, it stays red. There is no flashing, no second colour, and no cue that the grace period has expired. The contestant cannot tell from the lights whether they are at 7:10 or 7:40.

When the Clock Starts

Contest starts are far more precisely defined than club starts. After the introduction, the contestant proceeds immediately to the speaking position and says "Thank you, Contest Chair." If the audio-visual equipment is working, the chair replies "You're welcome."

Timing then begins with the contestant's next definite verbal or nonverbal communication with the audience. Usually that is the next word spoken — but the rulebook explicitly includes other forms of communication such as sound effects or a staged act by another person. A contestant who opens with thirty seconds of silent mime is being timed throughout.

If the audio-visual equipment is not working, the chair pauses and reintroduces the contestant. In an Online contest, a contestant with a technical problem gets 10 minutes to resolve it and is then either reintroduced or skipped.

The Two Timer Roles

A contest requires two timers, and they do different jobs — this is the most commonly misunderstood part of contest timing.

  • Timer one holds the stopwatch and completes the Speech Contest Time Record Sheet (Item 1175). That written record constitutes the official time.
  • Timer two operates the signalling device that displays green, yellow, and red.

Note what this is not: the two timers are not independent clocks to be cross-checked. Only one official time exists, and it is the one on the record sheet. The rulebook specifies no procedure for reconciling a disagreement, because by design there is nothing to reconcile.

Online contests additionally require at least one backup timer. If the primary timer suffers a technical failure they notify the chief judge immediately; the backup's record is consulted only from the point at which the primary was unable to continue.

Accommodations and equipment failure

  • Visually impaired contestants may request a warning signal of their own choosing — a buzzer, a bell, or a person announcing the times aloud — and the request must be granted. The contestant provides any special device required.
  • Equipment failure earns the speaker 30 seconds of extra overtime before disqualification. This applies only to technical failure of the signal or timing equipment; the rulebook does not extend it to other disruptions.

How Disqualifications Are Announced

Before announcing results, the contest chair must announce the number of time disqualifications but must not name the contestants involved. Nobody is publicly identified as having gone over.

Disqualifications on other grounds — originality, reference to another contestant or their speech, or failing to present an entirely new speech at the World Championship — are not announced at all. Eligibility disqualification sits with the contest chair; originality and reference disqualifications require giving the contestant a chance to respond and a majority of voting judges concurring.

The Evaluation Contest's Extra Timing

The Evaluation contest has a second timed element that the table above does not cover. It opens with a five- to seven-minute test speech. At its conclusion all contestants leave the room and have exactly five minutes to prepare their evaluation using the Evaluation Contestant Notes sheet (Item 1177), supervised by the contest Sergeant at Arms. After five minutes no further preparation is allowed and notes are handed in, to be returned as each contestant is introduced.

Two things the rulebook deliberately leaves unspecified: there are no signal timings or disqualification thresholds for the test speech itself (the 4:30/7:30 rule is written for contestants, not the test speaker), and no warning-signal scheme is defined for the five-minute preparation period.

Contest Timing vs. Club Timing

If you have only ever timed club meetings, four things change:

  • The disqualification window is not a flat 30 seconds either side. Club practice treats the grace period as 30 seconds past red. In contests the floor and ceiling are stated per contest, and the Evaluation contest's floor (1:30) sits a full 30 seconds below its required minimum (2:00) — so an evaluation of 1:45 is under length but not disqualified.
  • Fractions of a second are discarded, which works in the contestant's favour at the boundary.
  • Signals are held for a defined duration rather than flashed and dropped.
  • The Timer does not announce anything. Times go on the record sheet; the chair announces the count of disqualifications.

For ordinary club meetings, the simpler rules on the speech types and timing page apply, and the timing chart is the quicker reference.

Timing a Contest with the Toastmasters Timer

The Toastmasters Timer app covers the contest ranges through its speech-type presets, and displays a full-screen colour that satisfies the "clearly visible to the speaker" requirement while staying unobtrusive to the audience. It records exact times for the record sheet, and in online contests it can drive the signal through a Zoom virtual background so a remote contestant sees the colour in the timer's video window.

Two caveats for contest use. First, the app assists the timer holding the signalling device; the official time still belongs on the printed Speech Contest Time Record Sheet, completed by hand. Second, always confirm thresholds against the current rulebook before the contest — the rules are revised annually and this page reflects the 2026–2027 edition.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the timing rules for a Toastmasters speech contest?

International, Humorous, and Online: 5–7 minutes, disqualified under 4:30 or over 7:30. Table Topics: 1–2 minutes, disqualified under 1:00 or over 2:30. Evaluation: 2–3 minutes, disqualified under 1:30 or over 3:30. Tall Tales: 3–5 minutes, disqualified under 2:30 or over 5:30.

When does timing start in a contest?

After the "Thank you, Contest Chair" / "You're welcome" exchange, on the contestant's next definite verbal or nonverbal communication with the audience — including sound effects or a staged act, not only spoken words.

How many timers does a contest need?

Two: one with a stopwatch who completes the Time Record Sheet holding the official time, and one who operates the green/yellow/red signalling device. Online contests also require at least one backup timer.

What happens if the timing equipment fails?

The speaker is allowed 30 seconds extra overtime before disqualification. This applies to technical failure of the signal or timing equipment only.

Are disqualified contestants named?

No. The chair announces how many time disqualifications occurred, without naming anyone. Disqualifications on other grounds are not announced at all.

Why is the Evaluation floor 1:30 when the minimum is 2:00?

That asymmetry is in the rulebook. The required length is 2–3 minutes, but disqualification only applies below 1:30 or above 3:30 — so a 1:45 evaluation is under length yet not disqualified.

Do club grace-period rules apply in contests?

No. Use the per-contest thresholds in the table above rather than the club convention of "30 seconds past red," which does not match the Evaluation or Table Topics floors.
